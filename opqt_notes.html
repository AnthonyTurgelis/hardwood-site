Notes on this field

Olympic Pre-Qualifying Tournament, Guadalajara 2026 · back to the odds

Three things worth knowing behind the odds, none of which belong on the odds page.

The opener is a rematch with an asterisk on both sides. Brazil beat South Sudan by 15 in March — but Kamilla Cardoso scored 23 that night and isn't on this roster, while South Sudan's core from that game all returns. Our number discounts accordingly. Same logic runs the other way in Group A's headliner: Canada's 34-point March demolition of Argentina came with a WNBA rebounder who stays home this summer — though the leading scorer from that night is on the plane.

South Sudan is the team the rankings misread. World No. 42 by the table; continental bronze medalist on debut by the floor, within ten of China in March. When our number and the ranking disagree on a team, it's usually this team, and it's usually the ranking that's late.

Five of eight squads arrived as training pools. All eight are now certified rosters. The federations named their final twelves and the page re-baked on them, so every number here is built on the squad that will actually play. The Roster column on each group table names the source behind the count — New Zealand's certified ten is a ten, not a short twelve.

Projections publish before every tip and get graded publicly after every slate. Terms in the Glossary.

How the number was built

Team strength here comes from FIBA world-ranking points, converted to a scoreboard scale by fitting that gap against real results. The fit rests on the verified games listed in the fit sample table on the odds page. That is a small number and it belongs where you can count it rather than in a footnote: treat these margins as directional, not precise.

Why so few: a bulk scrape of this year's qualifying results returned a complete, plausible, and partly invented set of scores — one group's games were wrong in winner, margin and date. Every game in the fit was therefore confirmed one at a time against a governing body or a national federation, and nothing unconfirmed was used. The source that confirmed each one is printed beside it.

Some of those games were played by squads that are not the squads coming to Guadalajara. Brazil had its senior centre in March, Canada its senior forward, and neither was named for this tournament. Those games are counted at half weight — the weight column on the fit table says which — because they tell us about the federation, not about this roster. The expected scoring environment is fitted on the same games rather than borrowed from our league work.

The draw, and what Canada is bringing

The draw split the field's only two top-ten teams: Canada and Brazil land in opposite groups, so the modal final is the two of them and each group's real race is for second. Two tiers, one gap — Canada and Brazil, then daylight.

This is Canada without its senior core. The roster in Guadalajara is the programme's next wave, and our rating treats it that way: the measured column on the group table says how many of the named players are rated from measured professional production, with the rest from international-tier estimates. What the tournament answers is whether the depth chart behind the stars can win a must-win event on its own.

This field left its senior professional players home, so these ratings lean on international-tier estimates more than our league work does. Each row on the group tables says exactly how much is measured versus tiered, and the flag beside a team name links to what that means.

What we will not do

No betting market of any kind feeds these numbers. We measure ourselves against the market, never from it.

Court adds nothing to any call here: every game is at the same neutral arena. Mexico is at home in the building, but we have not measured a home effect in this competition and will not borrow the one we measured in a different league. Rest adds nothing for the same reason — we have no measured rest effect for this competition, and the effect we measured elsewhere is not ours to lend. Both parts therefore render as zero rather than as a guess.

The knockout bracket is simulated by format. FIBA has not posted semi-final tip times and we do not invent them. If the projection cannot be read at all, the page says so and shows nothing — an empty page is the correct answer there, and a stale one is not.
